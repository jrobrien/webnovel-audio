The Corvid Anselm had been dead for so long that even its distress beacon had given up. Mara Veketh matched rotation with it anyway, because that was the job, and because two hundred and eleven people were watching her do it.

"Depth check," Resk said over the comm, his voice thin with distance. "You're forty klicks past the last surveyed lane. If your rig quits out there, I am not coming to get you. I want that on the record."

Mara thumbed the reply without looking. "It's on the record. It's always on the record." She kicked off the Ladle's hull and let the gap close.

The airlock was a manual model, older than the war. Mara cranked it by hand for a full minute before the outer door sighed open on darkness. She went in.

Her socket woke as the lock repressurized. Suit integrity eighty-eight of one hundred. Air at forty-one percent, which was two and a half hours if she did not do anything stupid, which she intended not to.

The inner hatch let her onto the spine corridor, and something in the walls noticed her. A voice with no breath behind it told her the reactor was at nine percent and that she should not approach deck four.

She reached Bay 3 and stopped. The blast door was already open. Someone had welded it that way from the inside.

"Resk," she said quietly. "I do not think this crew left."
