1. Dead Air

voidwright

The Corvid Anselm had been dead so long that even its distress beacon had quit. It tumbled slow against the stars, and Mara matched her little ship to its spin, because that was the job, and because two hundred and eleven people were watching her do it.

Nothing this far out is worth what it costs to reach.

“Depth check,” Resk said over the comm, his voice thin with distance. “You’re forty klicks past the last surveyed lane. If your rig quits out there, I am not coming to get you. I want that on the record.”

Mara thumbed the reply without looking. “It’s on the record. It’s always on the record.” She kicked off the Ladle’s hull and let the gap close, and her stomach did the thing it always did when the tether went slack behind her.

The airlock was a manual model, older than the war. Mara cranked it by hand for a full minute before the outer door sighed open on darkness. She went in. Too easy.

Her socket woke as the lock repressurized, dumping a status card across the bottom of her vision.

Mara followed the corridor toward the bridge, boots ticking against the deck plates on their magnets, and tried to ignore the part of the readout that had started counting down.

Objective set by the last crew, and never finished. That was the part that always got her — not the bodies, not the cold, but the unfinished lists.

[ Objective, standing: seal the bridge. Priority: crew survival. ]

She reached Bay 3 and stopped. The blast door was already open. Someone had welded it that way from the inside.

“Resk,” she said quietly. “I do not think this crew left.”
